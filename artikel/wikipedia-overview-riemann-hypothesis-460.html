Topic Description: Statement about zeros of the zeta function

In mathematics, the Riemann hypothesis is the hypothesis that the Riemann zeta function has its zeros only at the negative even integers and at complex numbers with real part. First posed in 1859 by Bernhard Riemann, pure mathematicians consider the hypothesis to be one of the most important problems in the field.

It is important in number theory because it implies bounds on the distribution of prime numbers. The name is also used for some closely related analogues such as the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields, which was proved by André Weil around 1940.
